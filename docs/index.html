The gap

The Arabic cancer patient
was never part of the design.

90% of digital health tools are built in English, for Western patients and Western lives. But the gap is deeper than language. No companion existed — not in Arabic, not in any language — that understood her protocol, her culture, and what it means to be ill in this part of the world.

Clinical accuracy

Built around your
exact protocol

Rehlah is not a generic symptom tracker. The check-in adapts to your chemotherapy regimen, your cycle number, and your day — showing what matters right now.

AC-T
Doxorubicin + Cyclophosphamide → Paclitaxel
The most common adjuvant regimen for early-stage breast cancer. 8 infusion events across two distinct phases — each tracked differently.
AC phase — nausea, fatigue, vomiting
Nadir Day 10 — fever & infection priority
Taxol phase — joint pain, neuropathy
Recovery — energy, mood, function
TC
Docetaxel + Cyclophosphamide
Preferred when anthracyclines are contraindicated. Docetaxel-specific effects — fluid retention and joint pain — are tracked distinctly from AC-T.
Day 1–5 — joint pain, fluid retention
Nadir Day 10 — fever & infection priority
Recovery — energy, mood, function
CMF
Cyclophosphamide + Methotrexate + Fluorouracil
A 28-day cycle with Day 1 and Day 8 infusions. Rehlah is the only companion that tracks the second acute toxicity window unique to CMF.
Day 1–5 — nausea, mouth sores
Day 7–9 — second infusion window
Nadir Day 14 — fever & infection
How it works

From invite code to
first check-in in 60 seconds

1

Enter your code

Your care team or hospital provides an invite code. It loads your protocol, cycle, and day automatically.

2

Rate how you feel

A 60-second check-in with the symptoms that matter for your phase — not a generic list of 40 questions.

3

Get real guidance

Phase-appropriate notes, nadir alerts when your immune system is at risk, and clear escalation guidance.

4

Doctor sees everything

A pre-visit report is generated automatically. Your oncologist opens it before your appointment.
